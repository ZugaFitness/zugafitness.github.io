Can Yoga Help You Sleep Better? The Honest Science (2026)

Last updated: 29 September 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Yoga improves sleep by activating the parasympathetic nervous system, shifting the body out of stress-dominant "fight or flight" mode.
  • Gentle, restorative poses done 30 minutes before bed lower heart rate and core temperature, signaling the brain it is time to rest.
  • Consistent practitioners report falling asleep faster and waking up less, with measurable changes often seen within 2-4 weeks.

If you find yourself tossing and turning, unable to quiet your mind at night, you are not alone. The short answer is yes: yoga can significantly improve your sleep, and it does so via nervous system regulation.

The Cortisol and Melatonin Connection

Stress keeps our system alert. When we are stressed, our bodies produce cortisol, keeping us in a "fight or flight" state. This alertness suppresses melatonin, the hormone responsible for sleep. Breath-led movement, however, reverses this process. By focusing on slow, deliberate breathing and gentle stretching, we activate the parasympathetic nervous system (the "rest and digest" state), which naturally lowers cortisol levels and allows melatonin to rise.

3 Poses to Do Tonight

You don't need a grueling workout to see benefits. Here are three gentle poses you can try tonight:

  • Legs-Up-The-Wall (Viparita Karani): Lie on your back and rest your legs vertically against a wall. This passive inversion calms the nervous system and relieves tired legs.
  • Child's Pose (Balasana): Kneel, sit back on your heels, and stretch your arms forward, resting your forehead on the bed or floor. This posture promotes a sense of safety and inner calm.
  • Reclined Twist (Supta Matsyendrasana): Lying on your back, hug your knees to your chest, then drop them to one side while looking in the opposite direction. This gentle twist releases tension in the lower back and spine.

The Realistic Timeline

While the first night of practice often feels calmer, the profound benefits compound over time. For measurable changes in sleep quality—like falling asleep faster and experiencing fewer wake-ups—consistent practice over 2-4 weeks is typically required. It's not magic; it's a physiological shift that takes time to reinforce.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which style is best before bed? +

Slow Hatha or restorative yoga is best. You should never practice an active style like Vinyasa right before bed, as it can be too stimulating.

How long before bed should I practice? +

Aim for 30-60 minutes before you intend to sleep. This gives your body time to lower its core temperature and fully transition into rest mode.

Does breathwork help too? +

Yes - practices like Bhramari (humming bee breath) and slow diaphragmatic breathing significantly deepen the calming effect.
