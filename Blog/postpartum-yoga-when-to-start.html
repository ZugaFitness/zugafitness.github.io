Postpartum Yoga: When to Start, What to Do, and What to Skip (2026)

Last updated: 30 August 2026

Rebuild strength safely with gentle postpartum yoga

If you are navigating the early months of motherhood, starting postpartum yoga is a gentle way to reconnect with your body and rebuild strength safely. Childbirth is a profound transition that changes your physical structure, sleep, and emotional baseline. It is very common to feel fatigued and physically disconnected after delivery. This is a time for deep self-compassion, not physical pressure. Aggressive, high-intensity gym sessions are not what your body needs. Instead, you deserve a safe space that respects your healing timeline, helps you breathe easier, and slowly restores your core and pelvic floor vitality from the inside out.

Reviewed by Anusha, Certified Yoga & Zumba Instructor, Zuga Fitness

When Can You Start Yoga After Delivery?

The absolute rule of postpartum exercise is safety first. Before starting any physical routine, obtain explicit medical clearance from your doctor or OB-GYN. Every birth story is unique, and your healing timeline depends on your delivery. Deferring to your doctor is critical because starting too early can lead to complications, pelvic floor strain, or delayed healing.

Delivery Type Typical Wait First Practice
Vaginal delivery 4-6 weeks, after medical clearance Walks + gentle breathwork
C-section 8-12 weeks, after doctor clearance Breathing, pelvic tilts, seated alignment

For those recovering from a vaginal delivery, general guidelines suggest waiting 4 to 6 weeks before starting gentle postnatal yoga. This phase is for rest. During this period, your uterus is contracting and pelvic organs are settling back into place. Any movement before your 6-week checkup should be limited to walks and basic breathwork, once cleared by your doctor.

If you are beginning yoga after c-section, your re-entry must be even more gradual. A Cesarean is a major abdominal surgery that involves incising multiple tissue layers that require more time to regain their structural integrity. General guidance suggests waiting 8 to 12 weeks before introducing active physical postures. Your early practice must focus strictly on breathing, pelvic tilts, and alignment to avoid tension on your healing incision.

Benefits of Postnatal Yoga

Postnatal movement is a therapeutic practice designed to support your physical recovery and mental well-being during a highly demanding phase. Research suggests that yoga physically changes your body's stress response and promotes relaxation at a cellular level. This downregulation of your nervous system is vital for managing the high cortisol levels that come with sleep deprivation.

The physical benefits of a targeted postpartum practice include:

  • Gentle Core Rebuilding: Postnatal breathing exercises act as a gentle structural intervention to reactivate deep core muscles without strain.
  • Pelvic Floor Rehabilitation: Restorative poses and breathwork improve pelvic circulation, supporting tissue healing and stability.
  • Relieving Posture Strain: Nursing and carrying a newborn can cause a rounded upper back and neck pain. Yoga poses gently open your chest and mobilize your spine to reverse this strain.
  • Restoring Energy and Sleep: Deep breathing increases your lung capacity and oxygen intake. Research suggests that mindful breathing techniques may help reduce anxiety and improve sleep.

6 Gentle Postpartum Yoga Poses

Once cleared by your doctor, you can begin introducing these six gentle postpartum yoga poses at home. Move slowly and keep a high-quality yoga mat and props nearby to support your joints.

1. Cat-Cow Pose (Marjaryasana-Bitilasana)

How-to:

  1. Start on your hands and knees with wrists under shoulders and knees under hips.
  2. Inhale, gently drop your belly, lift your chest, and look up slightly.
  3. Exhale, round your spine toward the ceiling and tuck your chin.
  4. Flow between these movements slowly for 5 to 8 breath cycles.

Why it helps: This gentle flow mobilizes your entire spine, relieving upper and lower back stiffness caused by carrying your baby.

Caution: Keep your spine in a neutral tabletop position if you experience any abdominal pain or pulling.

2. Bridge Pose (Setu Bandhasana)

How-to:

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart.
  2. Press through your feet and lift your hips and lower back gently off the floor.
  3. Hold the lift for 3 to 5 breaths, then slowly lower down.

Why it helps: Bridge pose strengthens your glutes, hamstrings, and lower back, providing essential structural support for your pelvis.

Caution: Do not lift your hips too high in the early postpartum weeks to avoid abdominal strain.

3. Legs-Up-the-Wall Pose (Viparita Karani)

How-to:

  1. Lie on your back and swing your legs up onto a wall, letting them rest vertically.
  2. Relax your arms, close your eyes, and focus on slow, natural breathing for 5 to 10 minutes.

Why it helps: This passive pose drains tension from your legs, supports circulation, and triggers parasympathetic relaxation.

Caution: Slide your hips a few inches away from the wall if your hamstrings feel tight.

4. Bound Angle Pose (Baddha Konasana)

How-to:

  1. Sit tall on your mat, bend your knees, and press the soles of your feet together.
  2. Let your knees drop open. Sit on a folded blanket if your hips are tight.
  3. Hold your ankles, keep your spine long, and breathe deeply for 5 to 10 slow breaths.

Why it helps: This pose increases blood flow to the pelvic region and gently stretches the hips without loading your spine.

Caution: Use blocks under your thighs for support if you feel any groin strain.

5. Supine Spinal Twist (Supta Matsyendrasana)

How-to:

  1. Lie flat on your back, extend your arms in a "T" shape, and gently draw knees toward your chest.
  2. Slowly lower both knees over to the right side, keeping your left shoulder flat on the floor.
  3. Hold for 5 to 10 slow breaths, then repeat on the left side.

Why it helps: This gentle twist massages your abdominal organs and releases tension in your lower back and hips.

Caution: Keep the twist shallow in the early weeks; use a block or pillow under your knees for support.

6. Breath-Led Pelvic Floor Activation

How-to:

  1. Sit comfortably with your spine tall, or lie flat on your back with your knees bent.
  2. Inhale deeply, letting your abdomen expand naturally.
  3. As you exhale slowly, gently contract and lift your pelvic floor muscles.
  4. Inhale to completely release and relax the muscles. Repeat this mindful cycle 8 to 10 times.

Why it helps: This exercise uses the "science of breath" to coordinate your diaphragm with your pelvic floor, helping to safely rebuild pelvic muscle tone.

Caution: Ensure you are completely relaxing the pelvic floor on each inhale; do not hold your breath.

Trainer's note: As a trainer who works with new mothers every week, I know how hard it is to find even ten minutes for yourself between feedings and diaper changes. When you finally step onto your mat, let go of any expectations about what your body "should" be doing. Your practice right now is a sacred pause to breathe, rest, and offer your body the gentle, loving recovery it has earned.

Poses to Avoid in Early Postpartum

Understanding what to skip in yoga after delivery is critical to protecting your body from injury. In the early postpartum months, your body still contains high levels of relaxin, a hormone that loosens your ligaments and joints.

Avoid these movements and try the safer alternatives instead:

  • Deep Twists: Intense twisting poses put rotational strain on healing abdominal muscles. Instead, do gentle, supported twists like Supta Matsyendrasana with props.
  • Intense Core Poses (Plank or Boat Pose): Movements that create high intra-abdominal pressure can worsen abdominal separation. Instead, focus on Breath-Led Pelvic Floor Activation to rebuild your core safely.
  • Deep Backbends (Full Wheel or Bow Pose): Deep extensions can overstretch your vulnerable abdominal wall and strain your lower back. Instead, practice Setu Bandhasana (Bridge Pose) to build gentle lower back and glute strength.
  • Inversions (Headstand or Shoulderstand): Advanced inversions place an intense load on your neck, shoulders, and pelvic floor. Instead, practice Viparita Karani (Legs-Up-the-Wall) for restorative benefits with zero spinal load.

Yoga After C-Section: Special Notes

Recovering from a Cesarean delivery requires precautions because your abdominal wall has undergone surgery. Your approach to yoga after c-section must prioritize the healing of your scar tissue and deep fascia.

  • Incision Healing: Avoid poses that stretch or pull directly on your lower abdomen in the first 12 weeks. Focus strictly on pranayama (breathing exercises) and gentle neck and shoulder stretches while seated.
  • Gentle Re-entry: When you begin physical poses, start with low-impact options like Viparita Karani and Balasana (Child's Pose). Use blankets and pillows to cushion your abdomen and prevent pressure on your scar.
  • Listen to Your Body: If you feel any pulling or discomfort near your incision, stop immediately. Restorative, live online classes offer a safe environment because an instructor can observe your form and provide modifications.

FAQs

Postpartum yoga when to start? +

For a vaginal delivery, you can generally begin a gentle practice after receiving medical clearance at your 4 to 6-week checkup. If you had a Cesarean delivery, you should wait at least 8 to 12 weeks and consult your doctor before starting physical poses.

Can I do yoga 40 days after delivery? +

Yes, you can begin a gentle practice 40 days (about 6 weeks) after a vaginal delivery, once you have received medical clearance from your OB-GYN. If you had a C-section, wait longer and defer to your doctor's timeline.

Which yoga poses should be avoided after pregnancy? +

Avoid intense core exercises like planks, deep backbends, and deep spinal twists, as they can strain your abdominal wall. Advanced inversions should also be skipped in favor of gentle alternatives like Legs-Up-the-Wall.

Can postpartum yoga help with back pain? +

Yes, postpartum yoga can help relieve back pain by stretching tight back muscles and strengthening your glutes and core. Poses like Cat-Cow and Bridge Pose provide a structural intervention to counteract the strain of holding your baby.

Is online postpartum yoga safe? +

Yes, live online postpartum yoga is safe when conducted through interactive platforms like Zoom with real-time instruction. Having a certified instructor who knows your name allows for personal correction and safe modifications.

Final Thoughts

Your postpartum journey is a time for patience, cellular restoration, and gentle rebuilding. By choosing a mindful, safety-first approach to movement, you can support your physical recovery and find a calm center.
