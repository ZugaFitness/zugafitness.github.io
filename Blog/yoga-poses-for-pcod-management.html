Yoga for PCOS/PCOD: A Gentle Routine to Support Hormone Balance (2026)

If you are struggling with the symptoms of hormonal imbalance, practicing yoga for pcos can be a gentle, life-changing step toward reclaiming your physical and mental well-being. Navigating irregular cycles, sudden weight fluctuations, and persistent fatigue can feel incredibly lonely and overwhelming. Aggressive, high-intensity workouts often leave your body exhausted and frustrated. Real, lasting wellness does not come from pushing your system to its absolute limits under stress. Instead, a supportive, mindful approach that works with your body's natural rhythms can help you manage symptoms, find balance, and restore your natural vitality.

How Yoga Supports PCOS

Specific yoga practices are designed as a natural approach to improving your reproductive health, much like the focus in our live online yoga classes. These targeted sequences work on multiple physiological levels to help regulate hormones and alleviate PCOS symptoms:

  1. Managing Stress and Cortisol: Chronic stress triggers a surge of cortisol, which can disrupt your entire endocrine system. Research suggests that yoga physically changes your body's stress response and promotes relaxation at a cellular level. Reducing cortisol helps your body return to a balanced hormonal state.
  2. Improving Pelvic Circulation: Many yoga postures involve gentle compression and release of the pelvic region. This movement increases oxygen-rich blood flow to the reproductive organs, which may help support ovarian function.
  3. Building Gentle Strength: Unlike high-impact gym sessions that elevate stress hormones, yoga builds functional muscle tissue through low-impact resistance. This gentle muscle activation supports insulin sensitivity and metabolic health without overtaxing your nervous system.

By combining these elements, you create a supportive environment for your body to heal. In our global community, many students practicing consistently report noticing improvements within about 8 weeks.

6 Yoga Poses for PCOS and PCOD

Here are six essential yoga poses for pcos and pcod that you can practice safely at home. These postures focus on opening the hips, stimulating pelvic circulation, and calming your nervous system. Remember to use a high-quality yoga mat for joint support and keep props nearby to modify poses as needed.

1. Baddha Konasana (Bound Angle Pose)

  • How-to:
    1. Sit tall on your mat with your legs extended.
    2. Bend your knees, pull your feet toward your pelvis, and press the soles together.
    3. Hold your feet or ankles, keep your chest lifted, and let your knees drop toward the floor.
    4. If your hips are tight, sit on a folded blanket or place blocks under your knees.
  • Why it helps: This essential pcos yoga pose opens the pelvic region, improves circulation to reproductive organs, and helps relieve pelvic congestion.
  • Caution: Avoid pulling your feet too close to your groin if you have an active knee or groin injury.

2. Malasana (Yogic Squat)

  • How-to:
    1. Stand with feet slightly wider than hip-width apart, toes pointing out at a 45-degree angle.
    2. Bend your knees and lower your hips into a deep squat, keeping heels flat.
    3. Press elbows against inner knees and bring palms together at your chest.
    4. Keep your spine long and chest lifted. Sit on a block if your heels lift.
  • Why it helps: Malasana stretches the pelvic floor, strengthens the lower back, and stimulates abdominal organs, which supports pelvic circulation and digestive health.
  • Caution: Avoid this deep squat if you suffer from severe lower back pain or knee instability.

3. Bhujangasana (Baby Cobra Pose)

  • How-to:
    1. Lie on your stomach with legs extended, tops of feet pressing into the mat.
    2. Place hands flat under your shoulders, hugging your elbows close to your body.
    3. Inhale and gently lift your chest off the floor using your back muscles.
    4. Keep your gaze forward and avoid putting excessive weight into your hands.
  • Why it helps: This gentle backbend stimulates the abdominal organs, opens the chest, and strengthens the spine to help counteract desk-bound compression.
  • Caution: Do not practice this pose if you are pregnant or have recently undergone abdominal surgery.

4. Setu Bandhasana (Bridge Pose)

  • How-to:
    1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart.
    2. Place arms alongside your body, palms facing down, with heels close to your glutes.
    3. Inhale and press through your feet to lift your hips and lower back off the floor.
    4. Keep your chin tucked slightly and hold for 5 to 10 deep breaths.
  • Why it helps: This pose stretches the hip flexors, improves blood flow to the pelvic region, and stimulates the thyroid gland, which supports metabolic health.
  • Caution: Do not turn your head from side to side while in the pose to prevent neck strain.

5. Supta Matsyendrasana (Supine Spinal Twist)

  • How-to:
    1. Lie on your back, extend arms to the sides in a 'T' shape, and hug knees to your chest.
    2. Lower both knees to the right, keeping your left shoulder flat on the mat.
    3. Turn your head to look toward your left hand if comfortable.
    4. Hold for 10 slow, deep breaths, then return to center and repeat on the left.
  • Why it helps: This restorative twist massages the abdominal organs, releases lower back tension, and aids in digestion and detoxification.
  • Caution: Back off the twist if you feel any sharp pinch or pain in your lumbar spine.

6. Viparita Karani (Legs-Up-the-Wall Pose)

  • How-to:
    1. Sit with your hip close to a wall, then swing your legs up onto the wall as you lie down.
    2. Slide your glutes close to the wall, letting your legs rest vertically.
    3. Relax your arms by your sides, close your eyes, and breathe naturally.
    4. Hold for 10 to 15 minutes, allowing gravity to assist circulation.
  • Why it helps: This passive posture is excellent for yoga for hormone balance as it triggers the parasympathetic nervous system and relieves stress at a cellular level.
  • Caution: Skip this pose if you have glaucoma or severe neck issues.
Trainer's note: When working with clients who have PCOS, I always emphasize that your practice should feel like a deep sigh of relief for your nervous system, not another chore on your to-do list. If you feel your jaw clenching or your breath catching during any posture, you are pushing too hard. True hormonal healing begins when you learn to listen to your body and honor its boundaries rather than focusing on how flexible you look.

Pranayama to Add

Breathwork, or Pranayama, is a vital component of yoga for pcod because it directly influences your nervous system and helps lower stress. Two techniques can be practiced daily to support hormonal balance:

Anulom Vilom (Alternate Nostril Breathing)

Restores mental clarity and balances your nervous system.

  • How-to: Sit upright. Close your right nostril with your thumb and inhale through the left for 4 counts. Close the left nostril, open the right, and exhale for 4 counts. Inhale through the right for 4 counts, close it, and exhale through the left for 4 counts.
  • Practice: Complete 9 rounds of this cycle to find calm and focus.

Bhramari (Humming Bee Breath)

Uses vocal vibration to soothe a hyperactive mind and reduce anxiety.

  • How-to: Sit with your spine straight. Place your index fingers on the ear cartilage. Inhale through your nose, and as you exhale slowly, make a steady, low-pitched humming sound.
  • Practice: Perform 5 to 7 rounds, focusing on the vibration to release tension.

How Often Should You Practice?

Consistency is far more important than intensity. A gentle, 20-minute daily practice is much more effective than an exhausting weekly session. Aim to practice 4 to 5 times per week, focusing on restorative poses and calming pranayama.

Listen to your body's fluctuations throughout your cycle. During your menstrual phase, keep your practice extremely gentle, prioritizing restorative poses like Child's Pose and Legs-Up-the-Wall over active flows. Adapting your routine to your energy levels builds a sustainable habit that supports hormonal health long-term.

What Yoga Cannot Do

While yoga is a powerful tool for supporting hormonal balance, it is a supportive therapy, not a medical cure or a replacement for professional healthcare. PCOS is a complex endocrine disorder requiring a multi-faceted approach.

Yoga helps manage stress, improve insulin sensitivity, and support pelvic circulation, but it must be paired with nutritious dietary choices, proper sleep, and medical guidance. Think of it as a partner to medical treatments to support your resilience from the inside out.

FAQs

Which yoga poses are best for PCOS and PCOD? +

The best postures are those that open the hips, stimulate pelvic blood flow, and reduce stress, such as Bound Angle Pose (Baddha Konasana), Yogic Squat (Malasana), and Legs-Up-the-Wall (Viparita Karani). These poses help relieve pelvic congestion and soothe the nervous system.

Can yoga help with PCOS weight management? +

Yes, yoga supports weight management by building lean muscle tissue, improving insulin sensitivity, and reducing cortisol, which can help prevent stubborn abdominal fat storage. Combining gentle strength with stress-reducing pranayama supports healthy metabolic function.

How often should I do yoga for PCOS? +

For best results, aim to practice 20 to 30 minutes, 4 to 5 times per week. Consistency is key to regulating your body's stress response and experiencing lasting improvements in your energy and cycle regularity.

Is yoga safe for PCOD? +

Yes, yoga is highly safe and beneficial for PCOD when practiced mindfully. It is important to avoid pushing into pain, use props to maintain proper alignment, and consult your doctor before starting if you have any pre-existing injuries or health concerns.
