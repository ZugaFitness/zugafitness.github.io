Sitkari (Sheetkari) Pranayama: Cooling Breath Benefits & Practice

Learn Sitkari Pranayama to cool your body instantly, reduce anxiety, and balance energy. Step-by-step live yoga guidance inside Zuga Fitness.

Breathwork provides an immediate tool to calm the nervous system

When the temperature rises—whether from a sweltering summer day, an intense workout, or a stressful encounter—the body and mind desperately seek a way to cool down. In the yogic tradition, Sitkari Pranayama (often spelled Sheetkari) is the ancient antidote to heat and agitation. Known as the "Hissing Breath" or "Cooling Breath," this specialized breathing technique offers an immediate way to lower your body temperature, reduce anxiety, and restore energetic balance. At Zuga Fitness, we teach Sitkari in our live online classes as a powerful, on-the-go tool for instant physiological and mental cooling.

What is Sitkari (Sheetkari) Pranayama?

Sitkari translates to "that which causes the sound 'sit' or 'see'." Unlike most pranayama techniques that require inhalation through the nostrils, Sitkari involves inhaling through the mouth—specifically, through slightly clenched teeth. As air is drawn in through the moisture of the tongue and the gaps in the teeth, it instantly cools the saliva and the blood vessels in the mouth. This cooled air then travels to the lungs, dispersing a refreshing, cooling effect throughout the entire body.

Takeaway: Sitkari Pranayama is a unique breathing technique that uses the mouth and teeth to draw in cool air, immediately lowering body temperature and calming the mind.

The Cooling Benefits of Sitkari Pranayama

Sitkari is highly prized for its ability to quickly shift the body's internal state. It is an excellent practice for anyone seeking relief from physical heat or mental friction.

  • Instant Body Cooling: It is highly effective during hot summers, after a vigorous yoga practice, or for women experiencing hot flashes.
  • Anxiety and Anger Reduction: In Ayurveda, anger and frustration are associated with excess 'Pitta' (heat). Sitkari directly cools this excess heat, soothing an agitated nervous system and rapidly reducing feelings of anxiety or irritation.
  • Blood Pressure Regulation: By activating the parasympathetic nervous system (the rest-and-digest response), Sitkari may help lower high blood pressure and slow down a racing heart.
  • Dental and Oral Health: Traditional texts suggest that the practice strengthens the teeth and gums, although it should be approached carefully if you have existing dental sensitivities.

Step-by-Step: How to Practice Sitkari Pranayama

Sitkari is relatively easy to learn and can be practiced almost anywhere when you need a quick reset. Follow these steps for a safe and effective practice:

  1. Find Your Seat: Sit in a comfortable, upright position (such as Sukhasana or sitting on a chair with your feet flat). Rest your hands on your knees in Chin Mudra (index finger and thumb touching) and close your eyes.
  2. Prepare the Mouth: Gently bring your upper and lower teeth together. Do not clench them forcefully. Open your lips comfortably wide, exposing your teeth. Keep the tongue flat inside the mouth, resting just behind the teeth.
  3. The Inhalation: Inhale slowly and deeply through the gaps in your teeth. You will hear a distinct hissing sound (like 'sssss') and feel the cool air rushing into your mouth and down your throat.
  4. The Exhalation: Once your lungs are comfortably full, close your mouth entirely. Exhale slowly and smoothly through both nostrils.
  5. Repeat: This completes one round. Continue for 5 to 10 rounds, gradually building up to more as you become comfortable with the practice.

When to Avoid Sitkari Pranayama

Because Sitkari's primary function is to introduce cold air into the body, it is not suitable for every situation.

  • Cold Weather and Illness: Avoid this practice during the winter months, or if you are suffering from a cold, cough, asthma, or excessive mucus congestion.
  • Low Blood Pressure: Since it naturally lowers blood pressure, individuals who already suffer from hypotension should practice cautiously or avoid it.
  • Sensitive Teeth: If inhaling cool air causes pain in your teeth or gums, you can modify the practice by using Sitali Pranayama (rolling the tongue) instead.

Takeaway: Avoid Sitkari Pranayama during cold weather, if you have respiratory congestion, or if you experience severe dental sensitivity to cold air.
